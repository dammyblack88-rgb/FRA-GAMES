# THE GAME — Global Life Universe Vision

**Project status:** Working vision and product direction. The final public title is intentionally undecided. “THE GAME” is a temporary internal label, not a trademark or release name.

**Studio:** SimeonJr Studios  
**Core promise:** Choose a life. Live anywhere. Become anything. Build experiences other people can explore.

## 1. Product definition

THE GAME is a planned global 3D life-simulation and creator platform. It is one persistent player life expressed through compatible careers, activities, stories and adventures—not a collection of unrelated games with separate avatars and disconnected saves.

A player creates one character and can move between life experiences: everyday city life, football, wrestling entertainment, tactical missions, driving and exploration, family simulation, business, and optional superpowered fiction. Progress carries across experiences only where the rules explicitly allow it.

The game is designed in Nigeria for a worldwide audience. Lagos is an important starting city, but no country is the permanent centre or limit of the product.

## 2. Non-negotiable pillars

1. **One identity:** A stable player identity, character history and selected achievements across compatible experiences.
2. **One connected world model:** Cities, districts, properties, businesses, travel and social history share common data contracts.
3. **A life, not a menu of disconnected games:** Careers can change; the character and their broader story persist.
4. **Player creativity:** Accessible forms and visual tools let players author missions, characters, businesses, homes, tournaments and stories without writing code.
5. **Living places:** Residents, businesses and neighbourhoods have schedules, relationships, needs and histories that continue beyond the player's immediate view.
6. **Global cultural range:** Countries, languages, clothing, food, architecture, music and local customs are represented with care and research.
7. **Fair progression:** Paid cosmetics and convenience must not buy competitive dominance. Creator rewards, virtual currency and marketplace rules must be transparent.
8. **Original intellectual property:** Build original characters, code, maps, brands, stories, animation, sound and mechanics. Genre inspiration is acceptable; copying protected assets, characters, maps, music or branding is not.

## 3. The connected life experiences

These are experience families inside one universe, not separate products that must all ship together.

- **City life and open-world exploration:** Move through neighbourhoods, drive or use public transport, work, meet people, complete stories and explore.
- **Football career:** Begin in local street football, train, join clubs, compete in leagues and pursue international opportunities.
- **Wrestling and sports entertainment:** Create a persona, entrance and signature moves; train, perform and compete in original fictional promotions.
- **Tactical and emergency operations:** Take on carefully designed missions involving protection, rescue, investigation and fictional tactical scenarios. This is a game experience, not real-world tactical instruction.
- **Family and everyday life:** Build a home, form relationships, raise a family, manage time and make life choices.
- **Superpowered fiction:** Choose a hero or villain path in a clearly fictional power-fantasy experience.
- **Business and city development:** Create or operate shops, studios, services, clubs and property; build a career and influence the city.
- **Entertainment careers:** Music, film, acting, production and creator careers can become connected progression paths.

The examples above describe the long-term creative direction. Each experience requires its own design, production, testing and content budget.

## 4. Character creation and life start

A guided character creator should collect only what the game needs:

- Player handle and character name.
- Starting country, city and neighbourhood.
- Starting circumstances, such as low-income, middle-income or wealthy.
- Background or first career interest.
- Appearance, body type, hairstyle and clothing selections from licensed or original assets.
- Personality tendencies and long-term aspirations.
- Preferred interface language and accessibility settings.

The system generates a suitable starting situation, introductory objective and available opportunities. Starting wealth should be balanced and configurable by scenario; it must not imply that real-world wealth or status can be purchased.

Appearance options should be broad and culturally respectful. Avoid stereotyping people or assigning abilities, morality or personality based on nationality, ethnicity or appearance.

## 5. Global world structure

The long-term world may include curated city hubs across Africa, the Americas, Europe, the Middle East and Asia. Candidate examples include Lagos, Abuja, Accra, Johannesburg, New York, Los Angeles, London, Paris, Madrid, Dubai, Tokyo and Mumbai.

**Important production distinction:** “Global world” is the product vision, not a promise that the entire planet will exist as one seamless, fully detailed map at launch. Build a scalable world made of connected city and district regions. Travel can move between regions through authored transitions, with loading hidden by airport, train, boat or other travel sequences where practical.

Each city hub should have:
- Recognisable but carefully researched districts and landmarks.
- Local transport, services, public spaces and architecture.
- Residents, businesses, jobs, missions and events.
- Region-specific content and language options.
- A shared coordinate, identity, inventory and economy model where compatible.

Do not reproduce private residences or sensitive locations as exact navigable targets. Use fictionalised or appropriately licensed representations when needed.

## 6. Persistent player life

The long-term life simulation can include:
- Daily schedules, work, training, rest, food and energy.
- Jobs, career progression, salary, expenses and savings.
- Homes, vehicles, property and business ownership.
- Friendships, family, partners, children and generational legacy.
- Reputation, fame, trust and public history.
- Health and wellbeing systems designed for gameplay, without pretending to provide medical advice.
- Consequences and recovery paths: setbacks should create new choices, not simply end a player's story.

Time, needs and consequences must be tuned so that the game remains enjoyable. Real-time obligations, punitive energy timers and manipulative retention loops are not required.

## 7. Player creator platform — forms, not code

Players should be able to create content through guided forms and visual editors. A form creates a structured draft; it does not instantly publish arbitrary content into every player's world.

Planned creator tools:

### Mission creator
Fields: title, description, story, region, permitted location, objectives, characters, dialogue, difficulty, rewards and content rating.

### Character creator
Fields: name, role, appearance assembled from approved assets, clothing, voice option, abilities permitted by the selected experience, biography and content rating.

### Business creator
Fields: name, category, district, services, products, prices, staff roles, opening hours and visual style.

### Home and district creator
Fields: permitted plot, building template, room layout, furniture, access settings and visitor permissions.

### Story and tournament creator
Fields: title, chapters or rounds, prerequisites, teams/participants, rules, rewards, age rating and attribution.

### Publishing pipeline
1. Save a private draft.
2. Validate required fields, references, balance and asset permissions.
3. Run automated text and asset safety checks.
4. Apply human review or community reporting for higher-risk content.
5. Publish to a limited test audience or moderated catalogue.
6. Collect ratings and reports; support takedown, appeals and version rollback.
7. Feature eligible creations based on quality, safety, originality and player feedback.

Player creations should not automatically become NPCs, missions or permanent public assets in other players' worlds. Cross-world sharing requires explicit publishing, moderation, permissions and versioning. Players retain appropriate attribution and must grant clear licences for the uses they choose.

## 8. Economy and creator rewards

The world may support virtual currency, jobs, property, businesses, clubs, investments and a creator marketplace.

- Keep the in-game economy separate from real financial markets.
- Stocks and crypto, if represented, are simulated fictional instruments—not real investment access or financial advice.
- Virtual currency is not cash and must not be advertised as guaranteed real-world income.
- Any creator revenue share, including a proposed percentage, remains a future commercial policy decision. It requires fraud controls, eligibility rules, clear terms, age safeguards, tax/payment review and a sustainable funding model.
- No real-money cash-out or paid creator publishing fee should be promised until legal, payment, moderation and operational requirements are proven.
- Paid cosmetics must not grant unfair competitive power.
- Use server-authoritative ledgers, transaction IDs, idempotency and audit logs before any online marketplace or paid transaction exists.

## 9. Multiplayer and social safety

Multiplayer is a later milestone, not a prerequisite for proving the core life loop. Before launch, design for:
- Server-authoritative ownership, currency, inventory and progression.
- Permissions for property, businesses and user-created content.
- Reporting, blocking, moderation and appeals.
- Privacy controls and age-appropriate defaults.
- Anti-cheat and abuse monitoring.
- Safe voice/text communication and user-generated-content controls.
- Data minimisation, retention policies and account recovery.

Do not expose real-world addresses, private contact details or sensitive personal information through player profiles or creations.

## 10. Delivery phases

### Phase 0 — Foundation and proof
Maintain the existing engine-independent gameplay core and compact browser 3D validation slice. Establish identity, life history, world clock, NPC schedules, relationships, memory, missions, inventory and save contracts. Verify automated tests and keep the prototype honest about its limitations.

### Phase 1 — One excellent city-life slice
Build a small, polished, original district with character creation, movement, a few meaningful jobs, a home, transport, NPC routines, conversations, one or two missions and a saveable life. Prove that the player wants to return to this world.

### Phase 2 — Expand the life
Add more neighbourhood systems, family, business ownership, property, vehicles and a deeper economy. Add one additional city region only after the first is stable.

### Phase 3 — First specialist career
Choose one additional experience—such as football—based on prototype quality and feasibility. Reuse the same character identity, world rules and progression contracts.

### Phase 4 — Creator tools
Start with safe, constrained mission and story forms using approved assets and fixed templates. Add character, business and home creation only after moderation, permissions and persistence work.

### Phase 5 — Multiplayer and global expansion
Introduce small-scale multiplayer, shared places and creator publishing. Expand to further city hubs, then additional career families such as wrestling, tactical operations and superpowered fiction.

Phases may be reordered after playtests, but scope must remain incremental. Do not develop every mode, every country or an unrestricted creator marketplace simultaneously.

## 11. Technical principles

- Long-term native production target: Unreal Engine 5.8, subject to version and hardware verification.
- The existing browser 3D MVP is a temporary, low-cost validation client; it is not the final native game.
- Keep gameplay rules engine-independent where practical so browser and Unreal clients can share tested contracts.
- Separate client presentation from authoritative game state.
- Use versioned schemas and explicit migrations for identity, saves, world events and creator content.
- Design region streaming and asset budgets for modest hardware as well as high-end systems.
- Use original or properly licensed assets and retain licence records.
- Build online services only when the offline/single-player loop proves value.
- No paid hosting, cloud compute, APIs, assets, subscriptions, credits or upgrades without explicit founder approval.
- Never claim a feature is playable, online, secure, compiled or deployed unless it has actually been verified.

2. Strengthen the reusable life-profile and character progression data model.
3. Extend NPC simulation with explicit homes, jobs, needs and daily routines.
4. Connect NPC memory and relationships to world events without duplicating state.

6. Keep the compact browser prototype playable and testable while the Unreal runtime remains a separate verification milestone.

## 13. What success looks like

A new player can create a character, choose a starting place and background, experience a meaningful first day, meet memorable residents, earn or spend virtual money, make a lasting choice, save their progress and return to find the world recognises them.

Later, that same character can pursue another career, travel to another city, build a business or play a story created by another player—without losing the life they have built.

**The ambition is enormous. The first playable slice must be small, coherent and genuinely fun.**


## 14. Living platform, social world and community evolution

The product is intended to be more than a finite game release. It is a continuously maintained and expandable world whose evolution is led jointly by SimeonJr Studios and its player/creator community.

- SimeonJr Studios maintains the core game, world rules, infrastructure, security, moderation and official experiences.
- Players contribute approved missions, stories, characters, businesses, builds, events, tournaments and other experiences through accessible creator tools.
- Community feedback, ratings, reports and participation help inform future updates.
- NPCs continue their bounded simulated lives and pursue goals when the player is away.
- New official cities, careers, features and creator tools are introduced in staged updates as engineering capacity and funding allow.
- Preserve backward compatibility and save migrations so updates do not casually erase player histories or creations.
- The ambition is a world that can keep growing; no software service can honestly promise to operate forever. Sustainability, backups, security maintenance and operational funding are part of the design.

## 15. Social communication, streaming and real-device companion

A connected social layer is part of the long-term platform direction:

- Text chat, direct messages, groups, friends, community spaces and event coordination.
- Voice communication with user controls, mute/block/report, consent and age-appropriate defaults.
- Player livestreaming and broadcasts for gameplay, creator showcases, tournaments, performances and community events.
- Streamer tools such as moderation controls, audience settings, reporting, comment management and creator analytics.
- A virtual smartphone inside the game, with fictional in-world contacts, NPC conversations, game businesses, maps, mission messages and other original game applications.
- An optional real-device companion layer may surface permitted notifications and supported actions from services such as WhatsApp, calls and music controls while the player remains in-game.
- Real-service connectivity must use official APIs, operating-system notification/action capabilities and explicit user permission. Never scrape private accounts, bypass app protections, read message databases, or promise unsupported send/reply features.
- Clearly separate fictional game communications and balances from real messages, accounts, payments and identity.
- Support focus modes, quiet hours, notification allowlists, previews, mute and privacy controls. Do not expose private message content to other players or to the game backend unless a user explicitly invokes a supported feature that requires it.
- Platform capabilities differ by operating system and service provider. WhatsApp quick replies, voice messages, calls and other actions are conditional on official integration and device permissions; do not advertise universal support before verifying it.

## 16. Real creator economy and SimeonJr investment

The platform should create legitimate opportunities for talented players and creators to earn real-world rewards, while keeping virtual gameplay money separate from real money.

Potential creator activities include original experiences, approved digital creations, videos, livestreams, events, tournaments, sponsored challenges and other eligible work. Future reward forms may include cash payouts, airtime, data bundles, sponsored prizes, production support or grants.

A proposed **SimeonJr Creator Fund** may allocate a defined budget to selected creators and community projects. The studio may invest directly in promising creator work when it has an approved, available budget. This is a proposal, not an existing fund or a promise of guaranteed earnings.

Possible funding sources, subject to commercial validation, include platform revenue, sponsorships, approved marketplace commissions, paid events and explicit studio investment. Creator rewards must be backed by real funds or funded sponsor commitments. Do not display fictional earnings as withdrawable money or promise cash-outs before payment operations exist.

Before any real-money programme launches, define:
- Published eligibility, quality, originality and selection criteria.
- Clear reward terms, budgets, payout schedules and dispute processes.
- Identity and payment verification proportionate to risk.
- Age safeguards and guardian/consent requirements where applicable.
- Fraud, bot, engagement manipulation, collusion and money-laundering controls.
- Copyright, licence, attribution and takedown processes.
- Nigerian and relevant international payment, tax, consumer-protection and privacy compliance.
- Auditable reward ledgers, approvals, transaction IDs and reconciliation.
- Transparent sponsorship disclosures and separation of paid promotion from organic rankings.

Do not launch pay-to-win systems. Do not make real-world rewards contingent on performing real-world criminal acts. Fictional heists, fictional crime stories and virtual mission payouts may exist as entertainment within the game, but they are not real earning methods. Any simulated stocks or crypto remain fictional game instruments, not real financial access or advice.

## 17. Platform sustainability and governance

A long-lived community platform needs a funded operating model and clear governance:
- Plan for server, bandwidth, storage, moderation, customer support, security, backups and ongoing development costs.
- Launch social and streaming capabilities in stages; live video is bandwidth- and moderation-intensive and must not be treated as a free feature at scale.
- Establish community rules, creator terms, intellectual-property licences, privacy notices and enforcement/appeal processes before public creator publishing.
- Give users controls over discoverability, messages, livestream audiences, personal data and account safety.
- Provide reporting, blocking, moderation queues, appeals, takedowns and emergency response procedures.
- Build age-appropriate experiences and safeguards; do not expose minors to unrestricted live contact, monetisation or public broadcasting.
- Maintain incident response, backup/restore drills, security updates and service continuity planning.
- Keep creator content portable/exportable where practical and document service limitations to reduce lock-in.

These systems are product requirements for future phases, not claims that social networking, livestreaming, WhatsApp integration, payouts or a creator fund are currently implemented.
